Project Description

This project focuses on both the acclimation rate and capacity of Skistodiaptomus pallidus, a freshwater species of calanoid copepod. This project will take place in two different parts. In the first part we will compare CTmax data of a control group (16C) and a warm group (22C), as well as how this differs between two different populations; Ochsner Pond and Centennial Park. In the second part of this project we will do a transcriptomic analysis of the different populations and temperature treatments and compare the expression and regulation of certain genes by looking at differences in RNA.

Questions, Objectives, Hypotheses

Questions: 1. How does the magnitude/capacity of acclimation compare across populations? 2. How does the rate of acclimation compare across populations? 3. What is the genetic basis for changes in thermal limits?

Objectives: 1. Compare CTmax between the two treatments and populations to determine the magnitude of acclimation. 2. Track the CTmax over shorter timescales to compare the rate of acclimation between the two populations. 3. Determine relationships between the frequency of certain RNA strands in response to heat acclimation. Refine<<

Hypothesis: 1. The magnitude of acclimation will be higher in the population with a higher annual maximum temperatures. 2. The rate of acclimation will be lower in the population with a higher magnitude of acclimation. 3. We would expect the population with a higher magnitude of acclimation to have a larger change in differentially expressed genes between the two treatments.

Statistical Tests of Hypotheses

Hypothesis 1: - Tests of assumptions:

  • How model fit will be evaluated:

  • Post-hoc tests: Because this hypothesis is only comparing two groups, a post-hoc test is not applicable.

  • Non-standard analyses:

  • Caveats/Caution when interpreting results: Causality: Significant differences between the CTmax of the two populations would show a correlation between the magnitude of acclimation and the population/sample site but would not show causality since unmeasured factors could influence outcomes. Generalizations: Findings from this experiment can not be used to generalize other freshwater calanoid copepods, or other species living in either population, but can be used to draw implications. Generation: Both populations have spent enough time in lab conditions that they are >F3, this could effect results, but does not diminish or weaken them, but should be made clear nonetheless.

  • Handling missing values: CTmax: Missing (“NA”) CTmax values will not be used for data analyses, CTmax values that are recorded under ~30C will not be used. Non-CTmax data: Missing data other than CTmax data will interfere with results if used, so we will not use that either.
